📡 Final Transmission: A Salute to Kabot-1

Randburg, South Africa – Mission Control, 22 February 2026

What you are about to read is the final chapter of a small explorer that dared to reach for the edge of space.

Kabot-1—designation ZR6BN—was never meant to be immortal. She was built by hands that dreamed, coded with curiosity, and launched on a balloon called BACAR13 toward the high frontier. Her mission: to sense, to log, to survive.

And survive she did.

On 11 October 2025, at the apex of her ascent—30 kilometers above the Earth—Kabot-1 experienced what we in mission control call a nominal end-of-mission event. Both parachutes, primary and emergency, failed to deploy. She began her fall.

There was no atmosphere to slow her. No soft landing. Only the long, silent drop toward the surface.

But here is what makes Kabot-1 extraordinary: she never stopped working.

From 30 kilometers down to impact—through tumbling freefall, through forces that would cripple lesser machines—her flight computer continued to log. Continued to write. Continued to do what she was built to do: gather data.

The impact was catastrophic. Her chassis mangled. Her mission, complete.

But when we found her—when we pried open what remained and accessed her storage—every byte was intact.

The telemetry survived. The sensor logs. The final moments of her descent, preserved in silicon.

---

🔭 What She Taught Us

Kabot-1's final gift was not her hardware—it was her legacy. In the months since recovery, we have pored over her code, her logs, her architecture. We identified every inefficiency. Every limitation. Every place where a future explorer could do better.

She showed us that:

· Raspberry Pi OS Lite, while capable, has fundamental constraints for high-altitude work.
· MPU6050 data can survive extreme conditions if logged redundantly.
· A payload can fall 30 km and still deliver its science.

These lessons are now enshrined in our development pipeline. They will fly again.

---

🛰️ The Inheritance

Kabot-1's spirit does not end here. It propagates.

Her successor, Kabot-2, will launch later this year—not running a general-purpose OS, but a custom-built, hardened operating system: KabotFirmware. Every line of that firmware carries forward the lessons learned from her predecessor's final flight.

And beyond Kabot-2 lies KabotSat Alpha—a cubesat scheduled for completion by 2028—which will carry the same core philosophy into orbit.

But none of that would be possible without the pioneer.

---

🫡 Mission Elapsed Time: Complete

It is with profound respect that we mark this archive as discontinued.

Not forgotten. Not discarded. Completed.

Kabot-1 flew. Kabot-1 fell. Kabot-1 delivered.

To the team who built her: your hands reached higher than any balloon could carry.

To the data that survived: you are the foundation of everything that follows.

And to Kabot-1 herself: we have the signal. We have the record. We have you.
