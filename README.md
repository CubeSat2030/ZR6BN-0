Randburg, South Africa – Mission Control, 22 February 2026

What you are about to read is the first chapter of a small explorer that dared to reach for the edge of space.

Kabot-0 designation ZR6BN—was never meant to be immortal. She was built by hands that dreamed, coded with curiosity, and launched on a balloon called BACAR13 toward the high frontier. 
Her mission: to sense, to log, to survive.

And survive she did.

On 11 October 2025, at the apex of her ascent—30 kilometers above the Earth, Kabot-0 experienced a unexpected turn of events. 
Both parachutes, primary and emergency, failed to deploy. She began her fall.

There was no atmosphere to slow her. No soft landing. Only the long, silent drop toward the surface.

But here is what makes Kabot-0 extraordinary: she never stopped working.

From 30 kilometers down to impact—through tumbling freefall, through forces that would cripple lesser machines, vital components such as her flight computer and her internal components were nominal. Continued to write. Continued to do what she was built to do: gather data.

The impact was catastrophic. Her chassis mangled. Her mission, is far from over.

But when we found her—when we were amazed on the minimal damage she had from the fall.

---

🔭 What She Taught Us

In the months since recovery, We rebuilt Kabot-0 from parts that survived the fall and a few spare parts to make up those that were destroyed. Since her rebuild we have pored over her code, her logs, her architecture. We identified every inefficiency. Every limitation. Every place where a future explorer could do better.

She showed us that:

· Our design though proven to be very robust can be improoved.

These lessons are now enshrined in our development pipeline. They will fly again.

Although Kabot-0 will never fly again due to her physical damage, we will use her as our guinnea pig to test KabotOS and payload module software during development.
